Frequently asked questions:

About the mars rovers:

Q: Which NASA mars rovers are still active?

A: Currently, as of 4/9/2025 (month - day - year), Curiosity and Perserverance are the only currently functional mars rovers by NASA. Sojouner was lost september 1997, Spirit had contact finally lost may 25th, 2011, and Opportunity sent its last status june 10th, 2018, and was finally considered lost by feburary 23rd, 2019, after hundreds of failed attempts to regain control.

Q: How many attempts have been made to mars, that failed?

A: Assuming you count missions that also fail at launch, around half of all missions to mars have failed - and it isn't surprising, rocket science is difficult, and a lot can go wrong, even with perfect planning. Technology can have issues on earth, where we can easily access it, and if something happens in space, there is little we can do to repair it.

Q: Which NASA Mars rover lasted the longest?

A: According to two sources, the Opportunity lasted the longest, at 14 years and 136 days - a little over 14 years and a third.

Q: Which NASA mars rover had the shortest life?

A: As you could probably guess from it being the first mission, the Sojourner had the shortest lifespan, of 83 days.

Q: What is the greatest threat to mars rovers?

A: Depending on if you count before landing, it would be the landing process itself - if anything goes wrong there, the rover could be destroyed. Once on mars however, multiple sources state that the degredation of mobility, instability and collision, mechanical failure, and moving too slowly are the greatest threats to rovers on mars.
